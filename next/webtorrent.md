## 1. Project health in 2026: version, cadence, maintainers, issues, license, bundle size, forks

### Takeaway
WebTorrent is alive but in bot-driven maintenance mode: v3.0.21 (2026-07-27) is the latest release, the v3 line is essentially a Node 22 bump plus dependency updates, and almost all recent commits come from Renovate and semantic-release. ## 2. Peer discovery in browsers: wss trackers, public tracker reliability, DHT, hybrid clients

### Takeaway
In a browser, WebTorrent can discover peers only through WebSocket trackers (which double as the WebRTC signaling relay); DHT, PEX, LSD, UDP trackers and uTP are all unavailable or stubbed out. On 2026-11-18 only three public wss trackers answered reliably in my tests, two were intermittent, and one that is still hard-coded into WebTorrent's and Trystero's default lists was dead, so a self-hosted tracker (or redundancy across several) is a practical requirement.

### Cited Findings
- WebTorrent's BEP table: DHT (BEP 5) is implemented in Node and "not implemented" in the browser; PEX (BEP 11) is implemented in Node and not in the browser (issue #1191); LSD (BEP 14), UDP trackers (BEP 15) and uTP (BEP 29) are marked "cannot be implemented" in the browser. - [docs/bep_support.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/bep_support.md)
- The browser build physically removes these modules: the `browser` field in `package.json` maps `bittorrent-dht`, `ut_pex`, `./lib/conn-pool.js`, `./lib/utp.cjs`, `net`, `http` and `@silentbot1/nat-api` to `false`. - [package.json](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/package.json)
- "In the browser, WebTorrent can only download torrents that are seeded by a WebRTC-capable torrent client." - [docs/faq.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/faq.md)
- Signaling mechanics in the tracker client: on each announce the WebSocket tracker client generates `Math.min(opts.numwant, 5)` WebRTC offers, with `trickle: false` (the full SDP including ICE candidates is produced before sending), and each unanswered offer times out after 50 s (`OFFER_TIMEOUT = 50 * 1000`). Reconnect backoff runs from 10 s to 1 hour. - [lib/client/websocket-tracker.js](https://github.com/webtorrent/bittorrent-tracker/blob/master/lib/client/websocket-tracker.js)
- Wire format, confirmed by live test: a JSON text frame `{action: 'announce', info_hash, peer_id, numwant, uploaded, downloaded, left, event, offers: [{offer_id, offer: {type, sdp}}]}` (20-byte binary strings for the IDs) is answered with `{action: 'announce', interval, complete, incomplete, info_hash}`. Measured for these notes against five public trackers. - [bittorrent-tracker](https://github.com/webtorrent/bittorrent-tracker)
- The reference tracker server supports HTTP, UDP and WebSocket ("WebTorrent trackers") in one process, each toggled by an option (`http`, `udp`, `ws`, all default true), has a `filter` hook to allow or deny info hashes, a `trustProxy` option, and a default announce interval of 600000 ms (10 minutes). The README says nothing about capacity. - [bittorrent-tracker README](https://github.com/webtorrent/bittorrent-tracker/blob/master/README.md)
- Public wss trackers in the ngosang list on 2026-11-18 (four entries): `wss://tracker.webtorrent.dev:443`, `wss://tracker.openwebtorrent.com:443`, `wss://tracker.files.fm:7073/announce`, `wss://open.ftorrent.com:443`. A search-engine summary of mirrors of the same list reported only the files.fm entries; the direct fetch is authoritative. - [trackers_all_ws.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ngosang/trackerslist/master/trackers_all_ws.txt)
- Live reliability test, measured for these notes on 2026-11-18 around 12:20 UTC, one vantage point, four announce attempts per tracker with a random info hash (Node 22 WebSocket, plus one curl upgrade handshake):
  - `wss://tracker.openwebtorrent.com` - 4 of 4 OK, announce interval 108 to 130 s, socket open 516 to 731 ms, reply 729 to 1039 ms after start.
  - `wss://open.ftorrent.com` - 4 of 4 OK, interval 120 s, socket open 221 to 479 ms, reply 278 to 603 ms.

  - `wss://tracker.files.fm:7073/announce` - 2 of 4 OK (interval 120 s); curl handshake returned HTTP 503.

- Dead or flaky trackers are still baked into defaults: `create-torrent` (used by `client.seed()`) ships `wss://tracker.btorrent.xyz`, `wss://tracker.openwebtorrent.com`, `wss://tracker.webtorrent.dev` plus five UDP trackers. - [create-torrent index.js](https://github.com/webtorrent/create-torrent/blob/master/index.js)
- Trystero's torrent strategy defaults (0.27.0): `wss://open.ftorrent.com`, `wss://tracker.webtorrent.dev`, `wss://tracker.openwebtorrent.com`, `wss://tracker.btorrent.xyz`, `wss://tracker.files.fm:7073/announce`, with default redundancy 3. `open.ftorrent.com` was added in 0.25.3 (2026-07-13). - [packages/torrent/src/index.ts](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/packages/torrent/src/index.ts); [Trystero releases](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/releases)

- libtorrent: the master ChangeLog lists "added support for WebTorrent" under the 2.1.0 release, with 2.1.1 and 2.1.2 marked released and 2.2.0 "not released"; the `RC_2_0` branch ChangeLog contains no WebTorrent entry. WebTorrent's FAQ describes libtorrent's support as "experimental (disabled by default)". - [libtorrent master ChangeLog](https://github.com/arvidn/libtorrent/blob/master/ChangeLog); [docs/faq.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/faq.md)
- The FAQ's list of WebRTC-capable clients is: WebTorrent Desktop, Vuze, Playback, webtorrent-hybrid, Instant.io, βTorrent, TorrentMedia. - [docs/faq.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/faq.md)
- `torrent.addPeer(peer)` accepts either an `ip:port` string or "a `simple-peer` instance (for WebRTC peers)", so peers obtained through any out-of-band signaling can be injected into a torrent without modifying WebTorrent. - [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md)

### Inferences
- Browsers cannot join the Mainline DHT because it is UDP/KRPC; the BEP table says "not implemented" rather than "cannot be implemented", which leaves room for a WebRTC-native DHT in theory, but nothing ships one. In practice a browser swarm has exactly one bootstrap mechanism: the wss tracker.
- Because browser PEX is not implemented, there is no tracker-independent way to learn about more peers once connected. If every tracker a swarm uses goes down, existing connections survive but no new peer can join. This is the single hardest centralization point of the stack.
- The tracker caps each announce at 5 offers and only re-introduces peers at announce time (108 to 180 s on the public trackers measured). A newly arrived peer gets up to 5 connections quickly; growth beyond that depends on later announces by itself or others. Mesh formation in a large swarm is therefore gradual, not instant.
- Non-trickle ICE means each announce waits for full ICE gathering for every offer before anything is sent, which adds setup latency compared with trickle signaling.
- The dead `tracker.btorrent.xyz` entry in three independent default lists (create-torrent, Trystero, bugout) shows that public tracker lists rot faster than libraries update them. Any design should pin its own tracker list and treat public trackers as a redundancy tier only.
- A Node `webtorrent` peer is the bridge between the two worlds: it can pull from TCP/uTP peers found via DHT and serve the same pieces to browsers over WebRTC, provided it announces to the same wss tracker the browsers use.

### Gaps
- Tracker reliability was sampled once from one network. No longitudinal uptime data for public wss trackers was found.
- I did not re-read the tracker server source in this session to cite exactly how offers are fanned out and answers routed (`to_peer_id`, `offer_id`); the description rests on the client source, the live announce test and prior knowledge of the protocol.
- I could not confirm which shipping libtorrent-based clients (qBittorrent, Deluge) enable WebTorrent support in 2026, or the release dates of libtorrent 2.1.x.
- Who operates `open.ftorrent.com` and `tracker.novage.com.ua`, and any usage policy for third-party apps, was not verified.

## 3. Extensibility: BEP 10, `wire.use()`, and what people built (bugout, P2PT, Trystero, others)

### Takeaway
WebTorrent exposes BEP 10 cleanly through `torrent.on('wire', wire => wire.use(Ext))`, and that is enough to run custom gossip over a swarm (bugout proves it), but every project that wanted realtime messaging and stayed healthy moved away from the full torrent client and kept only the tracker signaling. Of the three named libraries only Trystero is actively developed in 2026, and its 0.27.0 release already contains signed shared state, identities and a gossip overlay.

### Cited Findings
- BEP 10: a reserved bit in the handshake advertises support; extended messages use BitTorrent message ID 20, with extended ID 0 for the handshake and IDs greater than 0 assigned per extension through the `m` dictionary of the bencoded handshake payload. - [BEP 10](https://www.bittorrent.org/beps/bep_0010.html)
- WebTorrent's API: the torrent `wire` event is "Emitted whenever a new peer is connected for this torrent" and is the documented place to add "custom BitTorrent protocol extensions" via `wire.use(MyExtension)`. - [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md)
- bittorrent-protocol's extension API: "an extension can register itself with at a certain name, which will be added to the extended protocol handshake sent to the remote peer. Extensions can also hook events like 'handshake' and 'extended'. To use an extension, simply require it and call `wire.use()`." Known extensions listed are `ut_metadata` (BEP 9) and `ut_pex`. - [bittorrent-protocol README](https://github.com/webtorrent/bittorrent-protocol/blob/master/README.md)
- WebTorrent itself uses the mechanism three times in `lib/torrent.js`: `wire.use(utMetadata(this.metadata))`, `wire.use(utPex())` (Node only) and `wire.use(ltDontHave())`. - [lib/torrent.js](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/lib/torrent.js)
- bugout ("Browser-to-browser networking built on WebTorrent"): creates a full `new WebTorrent(...)` client and calls `wt.seed(blob, {name: identifier, announce})` so that every participant derives the same info hash from a shared identifier; identifies peers by NaCl signing keys (`nacl.sign.keyPair`); keeps a `seen` map of message hashes and forwards each first-seen message "to all connected wires" (flood gossip with dedup). Default trackers: `wss://hub.bugout.link`, `wss://tracker.openwebtorrent.com`, `wss://tracker.btorrent.xyz`. - [bugout index.js on jsDelivr](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/bugout@0.0.13/index.js); [chr15m/bugout](https://github.com/chr15m/bugout)
- bugout maintenance (checked 2026-11-18): last npm release 0.0.13 on 2022-06-21; last repository push 2024-05-08; 637 stars; 12 open issues and PRs. Not maintained in 2026. - [npm registry: bugout](https://registry.npmjs.org/bugout); [chr15m/bugout](https://github.com/chr15m/bugout)
- P2PT: "Simple library to establish P2P connections, communicate messages using WebTorrent Trackers (WebSocket) as the signalling server." An app identifier "is converted to a valid Info Hash and sent to our WebTorrent trackers who will give us a list of web peers". Features: JSON messaging and automatic chunking of long messages; works in browser and Node. It does not use the torrent client or the wire protocol at all. - [subins2000/p2pt README](https://github.com/subins2000/p2pt)
- P2PT maintenance (checked 2026-11-18): 1.6.2 published 2025-09-08 after a gap from 1.5.1 (2021-12-24) to 1.6.0 (2025-09-05); last repository push 2025-09-08; 653 stars; 7 open issues and PRs. Sporadically maintained, nothing in the 14 months before these notes. - [npm registry: p2pt](https://registry.npmjs.org/p2pt); [subins2000/p2pt](https://github.com/subins2000/p2pt)
- Trystero maintenance (checked 2026-11-18): 0.27.0 published 2026-11-11; six releases between 2026-05-26 and 2026-11-11; last push 2026-11-13; 2,861 stars; 3 open issues and PRs; MIT; one npm maintainer (dmotz). - [npm registry: trystero](https://registry.npmjs.org/trystero); [dmotz/trystero](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero)
- Trystero strategies in 0.27 (six): Nostr (default, package `trystero` or `@trystero-p2p/nostr`, redundancy 5), MQTT (`@trystero-p2p/mqtt`, redundancy 4), BitTorrent trackers (`@trystero-p2p/torrent`, redundancy 3), Supabase, Firebase, and a self-hosted `@trystero-p2p/ws-relay`. Switching is a one-line import change. No IPFS strategy is listed. Custom strategies are supported through `createTopicStrategy` (pub/sub backends; the docs name Cloudflare Durable Objects as an example) and a lower-level `createStrategy` for protocols "that exchange pre-generated SDP offers in bulk (like BitTorrent trackers)". - [docs/signaling.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/signaling.md)
- Trystero bundle sizes (project's own figures, Brotli, tree-shaken, `joinRoom` only): ws-relay about 41.5 KB, torrent about 42.6 KB, Nostr about 46.3 KB, Firebase about 81.6 KB, Supabase about 85.8 KB, MQTT about 122.0 KB. `roles()` adds about 5.2 KB and `scale()` about 12.5 KB. - [docs/signaling.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/signaling.md)
- Trystero privacy model: relays see only hashed room topics and encrypted handshakes; "Connection offers (SDPs) are encrypted with AES-256-GCM before touching any relay"; an optional room `password` derives the key with PBKDF2-SHA256 at 600,000 iterations. - [Trystero README](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/README.md)
- Trystero feature set relevant here: `makeAction` typed channels with automatic "serialization, chunking, backpressure, metadata headers, and transfer progress"; `addStream` for live audio/video; `maxReceiveBytes` receive budget defaulting to 256 MiB (0.26.0); persistent, unforgeable `userId` identities and signed JSON state primitives `makeState` / `makeUserState` (0.27.0). - [Trystero README](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/README.md); [Trystero releases](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/releases)
- Trystero torrent strategy behavior: since 0.25.4 (2026-08-30) it "now honors tracker-requested announcement intervals and keeps passive rooms registered at a low rate"; since 0.26.0 offers are created on demand instead of keeping prewarmed peer connections. - [Trystero releases](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/releases)
- Trystero runs outside the browser on Node >= 22, Bun and Deno with an `RTCPeerConnection` polyfill such as `werift`, and supports a `passive: true` always-on peer that backs up shared room state. - [docs/server-side.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/server-side.md)
- Similar projects: p2pcf (WebRTC signaling on Cloudflare Workers, positioned as an alternative to WebTorrent trackers that "may go down"; npm 1.3.14 on 2023-10-26, last push 2023-11-23, stale); PeerJS (1.5.5 on 2025-06-07, relies on a PeerServer broker); P2P Media Loader (Novage; uses WebTorrent trackers for HLS/DASH segment sharing). - [gfodor/p2pcf](https://github.com/gfodor/p2pcf); [npm registry: peerjs](https://registry.npmjs.org/peerjs); [Novage/wt-tracker](https://github.com/Novage/wt-tracker)

### Inferences
- There are two distinct ways to do messaging "on WebTorrent". (1) bugout style: run the whole torrent client, seed a dummy torrent, and ride BEP 10 messages on the piece-exchange wires. (2) P2PT / Trystero style: use only the tracker's announce and offer relay to bootstrap plain `RTCDataChannel`s. Approach 1 pays the full 223 KB bundle and inherits swarm semantics for a chat; approach 2 is what survived.
- A `wire.use()` extension only reaches peers in the same torrent swarm. Site-wide gossip therefore needs one well-known "lobby" torrent that every visitor joins (the bugout trick), in addition to one torrent per blob.
- For this application, option (b) "build on WebTorrent unmodified" is technically sufficient for gossip (custom extension on a lobby torrent), but the maintained ecosystem evidence points at option (c): keep the tracker protocol, drop the torrent client for realtime work.
- Trystero 0.27 overlaps heavily with the application's needs (gossip, signed state that outlives its author, binary transfer with progress, media streams, self-hosted relay, tracker strategy). The risk is the same as with the WebTorrent WebRTC layer: a single maintainer and a pre-1.0 API that had a breaking migration in November 2026.

### Gaps
- I did not re-verify in source that bugout registers its channel as a BEP 10 extension through `wire.use()` (my grep output was truncated before that part of the file). From prior knowledge it does, under an extension name like `bo_channel`; treat the name as unverified.
- No measurements of maximum message size or throughput for BEP 10 extension messages over WebRTC wires were found.
- I did not survey other 2026-era libraries beyond those named (for example CRDT-over-WebRTC providers); adjacent researchers may cover them.

## 4. Mutability: BEP 44, BEP 46, BEP 39, and workarounds

### Takeaway
None of the BitTorrent mutability BEPs is usable from a browser through WebTorrent: BEP 44 and BEP 46 need the DHT (absent in browsers and unimplemented in WebTorrent even in Node), and BEP 39 is not implemented at all. Mutable or "sticky" state has to be built above the swarm as signed pointers (public key plus sequence number plus newest infohash) carried by gossip, a tracker-scoped lobby, HTTP, or an HTTP-to-DHT relay.

### Cited Findings
- BEP 44 (storing arbitrary data in the DHT): mutable items are keyed by the SHA-1 of a 32-byte ed25519 public key plus optional salt (salt at most 64 bytes) and carry a 64-byte signature over the sequence number and value. "Storing nodes MAY reject put requests where the bencoded form of v is longer than 1000 bytes." "Without re-announcement, these items MAY expire in 2 hours. In order to keep items alive, they SHOULD be re-announced once an hour." Last modified 2017-02-01. - [BEP 44](https://www.bittorrent.org/beps/bep_0044.html)
- BEP 46 (updating torrents via DHT mutable items): the mutable item's value is a dictionary whose `ih` key holds "the 20 byte infohash" of the current torrent; consumers poll the item periodically; both publisher and consumers should periodically re-put it; the magnet form is `magnet:?xs=urn:btpk:[Public Key (Hex)]&s=[Salt (Hex)]`. Last modified 2016-08-14. - [BEP 46](https://www.bittorrent.org/beps/bep_0046.html)
- BEP 39 (updating torrents via feed URL): an `update-url` key in the info dict names an HTTP feed; the client issues a GET with the hex `info_hash`; for unattended updates the new torrent "should be signed" by the originator, identified by an X.509 certificate (ties into BEP 35 signatures). Last modified 2016-07-21. - [BEP 39](https://www.bittorrent.org/beps/bep_0039.html)
- WebTorrent support: BEP 44 "not implemented" in Node and browser; BEP 46 "not implemented" in Node and browser (issue #886); BEP 39 does not appear in the support table at all. - [docs/bep_support.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/bep_support.md)
- Issue #886 "Mutable torrents (BEP46)" has been open since 2016-08-17, has 45 comments, and was last updated 2023-06-25. - [webtorrent issue #886](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/issues/886)
- `mutable-webtorrent` ("Create / Load / Seed mutable webtorrents (BEP 46)") is a third-party wrapper; created 2018-12-24, last published 1.3.1 on 2020-05-12. Unmaintained in 2026. Its author's write-up on a BitTorrent-based P2P web describes the approach. - [npm registry: mutable-webtorrent](https://registry.npmjs.org/mutable-webtorrent); [A P2P Web Using BitTorrent](https://blog.mauve.moe/posts/p2p-web-with-torrents)
- WebTorrent's own FAQ on mutability: "Torrents are immutable. That means that once a torrent file is created, it cannot be changed without changing the info hash." Its suggested workaround for live content is a feed of successive torrents or info hashes that viewers follow, leaving the feed transport to the application. - [docs/faq.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/faq.md)
- A browser-reachable route to BEP 44 exists outside WebTorrent: Pkarr signs small packets with ed25519 keys and publishes them to the Mainline DHT; its `pkarr-relay` is "An HTTP-to-DHT gateway that lets browsers and other UDP-restricted clients publish and resolve Pkarr packets" (the relay performs the BEP 44 store). - [pubky/pkarr](https://github.com/pubky/pkarr)
- Trystero 0.27.0 ships application-level mutable state: `room.makeUserState` gives each participant "a cryptographically signed JSON slot keyed by their userId" that nobody else can overwrite; `room.makeState` is room-scoped state writable by any member (or restricted by roles) that is "cached and relayed to newcomers by remaining peers even after the author leaves". The docs warn that concurrent writes to the same shared `makeState` value overwrite each other. - [docs/state.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/state.md)

### Inferences
- The owner's "sticky" state maps well onto the BEP 44 data model (public key, monotonically increasing sequence number, signature, small value) even though the BEP 44 transport (the DHT) is unreachable. The same record can be gossiped over data channels or a `wire.use()` extension: peers keep the highest valid sequence number per key and hand it to newcomers. That is a signed last-writer-wins register, and it needs no change to WebTorrent.
- A BEP 46 style pointer (`{ih: <infohash>, seq, sig}`) carried by that gossip gives "mutable torrents" in the browser: blobs stay immutable and content-addressed, the pointer is the only mutable part. This is exactly the FAQ's "feed of info hashes" idea with a signature added.
- The 1000-byte BEP 44 limit is a useful design constraint to adopt voluntarily: keep sticky values tiny and put anything larger in a torrent referenced by infohash.
- Swarm-held state disappears when the last holder leaves (BEP 44 itself expires in about 2 hours without re-announce). Durable stickiness needs at least one always-on participant: a passive headless peer, a Pkarr-style relay to the real DHT, or simply a signed JSON file on the static host that seeds the newest pointer on page load.
- "State the swarm agrees on" beyond single-writer or last-writer-wins (multi-writer convergence, conflict resolution, Sybil resistance) is not provided by any BitTorrent BEP or by WebTorrent; it is an application-layer problem regardless of which option (a), (b) or (c) is chosen.

### Gaps
- Pkarr's maintenance status, public relay availability and rate limits in 2026 were not verified (GitHub API quota exhausted); only its README was read.
- I found no maintained 2026 project that implements signed mutable pointers specifically on top of browser WebTorrent. `mutable-webtorrent` is the only named one and it depends on the DHT.
- Trystero's exact conflict-resolution rule for `makeState` (ordering, clocks, tie-breaks) was not read in full; only the overwrite warning is confirmed.

## 5. Hard limits: connection caps, NAT traversal, setup latency, mesh size, background tabs, mobile

### Takeaway
The firm numbers are: Chromium allows at most 500 `RTCPeerConnection`s per page context, WebTorrent defaults to 55 connections per torrent, the tracker hands out at most 5 offers per announce, and full meshes are considered comfortable only up to roughly 7 to 10 peers before a partial-mesh gossip overlay is needed. Expect roughly 10 to 20 percent of peer pairs to need TURN according to practitioner and vendor figures, which are not independently measured for browser data channels.

### Cited Findings
- Chromium source: "The maximum number of PeerConnections that can exist simultaneously. `const int64_t kMaxPeerConnections = 500;`" - [rtc_peer_connection.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/peerconnection/rtc_peer_connection.cc)
- Field report of that limit: in the Snowflake WebExtension a loop creating and closing peer connections failed at 499, and closing or garbage collection did not immediately free the budget. Older community figures of 256 also circulate. - [Snowflake webext issue 71](https://gitlab.torproject.org/tpo/anti-censorship/pluggable-transports/snowflake-webext/-/issues/71); [BlogGeek: RTCPeerConnection per stream](https://bloggeek.me/webrtc-rtcpeerconnection-one-per-stream/)
- WebTorrent client option `maxConns`: "Max number of connections per torrent (default=55)". - [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md)
- Tracker client: at most 5 offers per announce, 50 s offer timeout, non-trickle ICE. - [lib/client/websocket-tracker.js](https://github.com/webtorrent/bittorrent-tracker/blob/master/lib/client/websocket-tracker.js)
- Mesh size guidance from Trystero: "For 2 to ~10 people ... a full mesh is unbeatable"; at "25, 40, or 60 people ... asking every laptop and phone to juggle dozens of simultaneous WebRTC connections starts eating CPU and sockets". Its experimental `scale()` keeps a full mesh up to 7 peers (default `maxDirectPeers: 6`) and above that keeps about 6 direct neighbors per peer and routes messages, RPCs and state across hops. - [docs/scaling.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/scaling.md)
- NAT traversal, practitioner figure: Trystero's docs state that STUN succeeds for about 85 to 90 percent of connections and "roughly 10-15% of real-world internet connections" (strict corporate firewalls, university networks, carrier-grade NAT, some VPNs) need a TURN relay; without TURN those pairs fail with `peer-connect-failed`. - [docs/troubleshooting.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/troubleshooting.md)
- NAT traversal, vendor figures (not independently verified, taken from search summaries): about 15 to 20 percent of connections need TURN (WebRTC.ventures, 2022); "roughly one in five" citing Chrome UMA (Metered). These vendors sell or integrate TURN. - [WebRTC.ventures: ICE in WebRTC](https://blog.webrtc.ventures/2022/04/ice-in-webrtc); [Metered: What is a TURN server](https://www.metered.ca/blog/what-is-a-turn-server/)
- NAT traversal, large peer-reviewed measurement on a different stack: a study of libp2p's DCUtR hole punching in the IPFS network (4.4 million attempts, more than 85,000 networks, 167 countries; accepted at ACM IMC 2026) found a conditional hole-punch success rate of 70% ± 7.1%, statistically the same for TCP and QUIC, with 97.6% of successes on the first attempt. This is not WebRTC ICE, but it is the best recent measured bound for decentralized hole punching without a relay fallback. - [arXiv:2604.12484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12484)
- Signaling latency, measured for these notes on 2026-11-18: WebSocket open to a public tracker took 221 to 1069 ms and the first announce reply arrived 278 to 1322 ms after starting the connection (five trackers, one vantage point). ICE gathering and DTLS setup come on top. - [trackers_all_ws.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ngosang/trackerslist/master/trackers_all_ws.txt)
- Announce intervals returned by public trackers in the same test: 108 to 130 s (openwebtorrent), 120 s (ftorrent, webtorrent.dev, files.fm), 180 s (novage). The reference server's default is 10 minutes. - [bittorrent-tracker README](https://github.com/webtorrent/bittorrent-tracker/blob/master/README.md)
- Background tabs in Chrome (since Chrome 88): timers in a hidden page are checked once per second; "intensive throttling" to once per minute applies only when the page has been hidden for more than 5 minutes, the timer chain count is 5 or greater, the page has been silent for 30 s, and WebRTC is not in use. WebRTC counts as in use when there is an `RTCPeerConnection` with an open `RTCDataChannel` or a live `MediaStreamTrack`. - [Chrome for Developers: timer throttling in Chrome 88](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/timer-throttling-in-chrome-88)
- WebRTC exposes the visitor's IP address to other peers; the WebTorrent FAQ has a section on WebRTC and VPN IP leakage. - [docs/faq.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/faq.md)

### Inferences
- The 500-connection page cap combined with per-torrent connections is the binding constraint for a design with many simultaneous torrents: each torrent is its own swarm with its own wires (default up to 55), so a tab seeding 10 torrents at the default could approach or exceed the cap. A design should either hold few torrents open at once, lower `maxConns`, or move realtime traffic to a single shared mesh. That per-torrent connections are not shared between torrents is my reading of the architecture (one wire is bound to one info hash by the BitTorrent handshake), not a quoted statement.
- With a per-pair direct success rate around 85 to 90 percent, a gossip overlay tolerates missing links: a peer that fails to reach some neighbors still receives messages through others. Bulk transfer suffers more, because a peer behind a strict NAT may reach few or no seeders without TURN. Web seeds (section 6) are the cheap fix for blobs; TURN is the only fix for realtime traffic.
- A page with an open data channel avoids Chrome's once-per-minute timer throttling but is still held to once-per-second timers when hidden. Heartbeats and gossip timers should be designed for 1 s granularity, and announce or keepalive logic should not assume sub-second timers in background tabs.
- Setup latency budget: roughly 0.3 to 1.3 s to reach the tracker, plus full ICE gathering before the first announce (non-trickle), plus the answer round trip. A first peer connection within about 2 to 5 s is a reasonable expectation, but this is an estimate, not a measurement.

### Gaps
- No primary, recent, browser-specific measurement of WebRTC data-channel NAT traversal success or TURN share was obtained; the 10 to 20 percent range is practitioner and vendor consensus.
- Mobile browsers: I found no primary source in this session on iOS Safari or Android Chrome behavior for background tabs, tab discard, or connection limits. It is widely reported that mobile browsers suspend backgrounded pages and drop their connections, but that is unsourced here and should be tested.
- Firefox and Safari per-page peer connection caps were not found.
- End-to-end time to first peer and time to a full mesh were not measured.
- Data channel message size limits and throughput were not researched.

## 6. Browser storage and streaming: chunk stores, service worker, persistence, keeping content alive

### Takeaway
Current WebTorrent stores pieces in the Origin Private File System by default (via `fsa-chunk-store`), falls back to memory where that API is missing, and streams media through a 1.3 KB service worker. Content availability is purely a function of who is online, so the standard cheap remedy is a BEP 19 web seed on static hosting or object storage (supported in the browser, CORS required), optionally plus one always-on Node seeder.

### Cited Findings
- Store selection in `lib/torrent.js`: `SUPPORTS_FSA = navigator.storage.getDirectory && FileSystemFileHandle.prototype.createWritable`; the default store is `FSChunkStore` in Node or in a browser with that support, and the import comment notes the browser build substitutes `fsa-chunk-store`. A custom `store` option overrides it. - [lib/torrent.js](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/lib/torrent.js); [package.json](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/package.json)
- `fsa-chunk-store` 1.3.0 uses `opts.rootDir || navigator.storage.getDirectory()` as its root, that is, OPFS unless a user-picked directory handle is passed. Last published 2024-08-04, single maintainer (thaunknown). - [fsa-chunk-store index.js on jsDelivr](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/fsa-chunk-store@1.3.0/index.js); [npm registry: fsa-chunk-store](https://registry.npmjs.org/fsa-chunk-store)
- Docs: `storeOpts.rootDir` is "(browser only) FileSystemDirectoryHandle - if supported by the browser, allows the user to specify a custom directory"; `storeCacheSlots` defaults to 20 pieces cached in memory; `store` and `preloadedStore` accept any abstract-chunk-store implementation; `skipVerify` and `bitfield` let a client resume from an existing store without re-hashing. - [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md)
- Persistence across sessions is the default in the browser: "If you don't want to retain data across sessions, make sure to manually destroy the torrent store when the page closes" (at `beforeunload` at the latest). `destroyStoreOnDestroy` deletes the store when a torrent is destroyed. - [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md)
- Older browser stores: `idb-chunk-store` last published 2021-06-03; `hybrid-chunk-store` last published 2024-06-28 and no longer the browser mapping in WebTorrent 3. - [npm registry: idb-chunk-store](https://registry.npmjs.org/idb-chunk-store); [npm registry: hybrid-chunk-store](https://registry.npmjs.org/hybrid-chunk-store)
- Streaming: in the browser `client.createServer({controller})` is required, where `controller` is a service worker registration ("Browser only. Required!"); the worker is the shipped `sw.min.js` (1,297 bytes) or an equivalent. `file.streamTo(elem)` "Sets the element source to the file's streaming URL. Supports streaming, seeking and all browser codecs and containers", and `file.streamURL` returns the URL. - [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md); [jsDelivr: webtorrent@3.0.21 dist](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/webtorrent@3.0.21/dist/sw.min.js)
- Other read paths: `file.stream()` (W3C ReadableStream, prioritizes needed pieces), async iteration, `file.arrayBuffer()`, `file.blob()`; piece selection `strategy` is `sequential` by default or `rarest`. - [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md)
- Live streaming: "WebTorrent cannot do live streaming out-of-the-box"; the suggested pattern is one torrent per roughly 10 s segment with viewers 10 to 20 s behind live. - [docs/faq.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/faq.md)
- Web seeds: BEP 19 is implemented in Node and browser; enabled by default (`webSeeds` client option); `torrent.addWebSeed(urlOrConn)` adds one at runtime; "In the browser, web seed servers must have proper CORS (Cross-origin resource sharing) headers so that data can be fetched across domain." A custom web-seed connection object can be supplied if it has a `connId`. - [docs/bep_support.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/bep_support.md); [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md)
- v3.0.0 included a web-seed fix: "calculate requests correctly for webseeds (#3001)". - [v3.0.0 release](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/releases/tag/v3.0.0)
- An always-on seeder can be a plain Node `webtorrent` process, since Node WebTorrent >= 2.3.0 speaks WebRTC natively. - [webtorrent-hybrid README](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent-hybrid)

### Inferences
- When the last seeder closes its tab, the swarm has no complete copy and new visitors stall until a holder returns. Because pieces persist in OPFS, a returning visitor can resume seeding without re-downloading, but only if the application re-adds the torrent on load; WebTorrent does not remember torrents between sessions by itself.
- A web seed turns the static host into a permanent seeder at zero operational cost: the same file URL that would have been a plain download is listed in `urlList`, and peers offload bandwidth from it when present. BEP 19 uses HTTP range requests, so the host must support ranges and send CORS headers; object storage with a CDN satisfies this.
- For the stated blob types (PDFs, audio files, small weight files) the web seed alone already guarantees availability. WebTorrent's incremental value is bandwidth offload and operation when the origin is unreachable, which should be weighed against its 223 KB bundle and connection cost.
- A "web radio" of pre-recorded files fits WebTorrent's sequential streaming via the service worker well. A true live radio does not: it needs the segment-feed pattern, which in turn needs the mutable pointer mechanism from section 4, or simply WebRTC media streams over the gossip mesh for small audiences.
- OPFS data is subject to browser storage quotas and eviction; calling `navigator.storage.persist()` is advisable. This is general platform behavior, not verified against a source in this session.

### Gaps
- I did not verify which browsers in 2026 pass the `createWritable` feature check on the main thread (Safari has historically lagged), so the share of visitors who silently fall back to the in-memory store is unknown.
- No source was found for OPFS quota or eviction behavior specific to WebTorrent use.
- I did not test whether service worker streaming works on iOS Safari in 2026.
- No measurement of web-seed versus peer throughput in browsers was found.

## 7. Self-hosting cost of the unavoidable parts: wss tracker, serverless options, STUN/TURN

### Takeaway
The unavoidable server parts are small: a WebSocket tracker (or any pub/sub signaling relay) and, for the 10 to 20 percent of peer pairs that cannot connect directly, a TURN relay. A tracker fits on the smallest VPS or plausibly inside Cloudflare's Durable Objects free tier for modest swarms, though I found no ready-made WebTorrent-protocol tracker for Workers; managed TURN costs about $0.05 per relayed GB.

### Cited Findings
- Reference tracker: `npm install -g bittorrent-tracker` then `bittorrent-tracker` (default port 8000); HTTP, UDP and WebSocket servers can be toggled individually; a `filter` function can whitelist info hashes; `trustProxy` supports running behind a reverse proxy for TLS. - [bittorrent-tracker README](https://github.com/webtorrent/bittorrent-tracker/blob/master/README.md)
- Higher-performance trackers: `aquatic_ws` (Rust, Linux 5.8+, used by tracker.webtorrent.dev); Novage `wt-tracker` (Node, uWebSockets.js), whose README now recommends its Rust port `wt-tracker-rust` as a drop-in replacement that uses all cores, 1.35 to 1.6 times less CPU per message and, in three of four load profiles, 3.2 to 4.6 times less memory per connection. - [greatest-ape/aquatic](https://github.com/greatest-ape/aquatic); [Novage/wt-tracker](https://github.com/Novage/wt-tracker)
- Deno option: a JSR package `@vanaware/browsertorrent-tracker` describes itself as a WebSocket and HTTP tracker for WebTorrent and WebRTC swarms (seen in search results only; not evaluated). - [JSR: browsertorrent-tracker](https://jsr.io/@vanaware/browsertorrent-tracker)
- Cloudflare Workers: no WebTorrent-wire-compatible tracker for Workers or Durable Objects turned up in a web search and two GitHub repository searches on 2026-11-18. The nearest projects are p2pcf (its own signaling protocol on Workers, stale since 2023) and write-ups that use one Durable Object per room for WebSocket signaling. - [gfodor/p2pcf](https://github.com/gfodor/p2pcf); [DEV: P2P file sharing with Cloudflare](https://dev.to/kiyoe/zero-egress-costs-how-i-built-p2p-file-sharing-with-cloudflare-4lhc)
- Trystero's docs explicitly list Cloudflare Durable Objects as a backend one can wrap with `createTopicStrategy`, and ship a self-hosted Node relay (`@trystero-p2p/ws-relay`, with built-in topic and subscription limits) for a VPS or container. - [docs/signaling.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/signaling.md)
- Durable Objects pricing (fetched 2026-11-18): available on the Workers Free plan with SQLite-backed objects only. Free: 100,000 requests per day and 13,000 GB-s of duration per day; operations fail when a limit is exceeded. Paid: 1 million requests per month included then $0.15 per million; 400,000 GB-s per month included then $12.50 per million GB-s. A request is needed to create each WebSocket connection; outgoing WebSocket messages and protocol pings are free; incoming WebSocket messages are billed at a 20:1 ratio. Objects that are idle and eligible for hibernation are not billed for duration. - [Durable Objects pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)
- TURN pricing: Cloudflare Realtime TURN "costs $0.05/real-time GB outbound from Cloudflare to the TURN client" and is free when used together with the Realtime SFU. - [Cloudflare Realtime TURN](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/)
- STUN and TURN roles: STUN only discovers public addresses and carries no traffic; TURN relays encrypted packets only for the pairs that cannot connect directly; Trystero ships built-in STUN servers and takes TURN via `turnConfig`. WebTorrent takes ICE servers via the tracker option `rtcConfig` ("RTCPeerConnection config object (only used in browser)"). - [docs/troubleshooting.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/troubleshooting.md); [bittorrent-tracker README](https://github.com/webtorrent/bittorrent-tracker/blob/master/README.md)

### Inferences
- Tracker load is tiny. Worked example: 1,000 concurrent peers announcing every 120 s produce 720 announces per peer per day, 720,000 incoming messages per day in total. At the Durable Objects 20:1 ratio that is 36,000 billable requests per day, under the 100,000 per day free allowance; per month it is about 21.6 million messages, or 1.08 million billable requests, which on the paid plan is the included million plus roughly one cent.
- Duration is the real Durable Objects cost driver: a swarm busy enough that its object never hibernates is billed wall-clock. Assuming the commonly documented 128 MB per active object (not re-verified in this session), one always-awake object is about 11,060 GB-s per day (0.128 GB times 86,400 s), under the 13,000 GB-s free daily limit but leaving room for only one such object; on the paid plan about 331,800 GB-s per month fits inside the included 400,000. One object per info hash keeps small swarms hibernating most of the time.
- Writing a WebTorrent-compatible tracker for a Durable Object is a small job: the protocol is JSON over WebSocket with one announce message type plus offer and answer forwarding, and the object needs only an in-memory map of peer ID to socket per info hash. The absence of an off-the-shelf implementation is a gap in the ecosystem, not a sign of difficulty. Being wire-compatible keeps public trackers usable as fallback.
- TURN cost scales with relayed bytes only. Example: a 128 kbit/s audio stream relayed for one hour is 57.6 MB, about $0.003 at $0.05 per GB. Relaying gossip is negligible; relaying bulk blob transfer is where cost would appear, which is another argument for web seeds for blobs.
- Self-hosted coturn on a small VPS is the no-vendor alternative; it needs a public IP and UDP, so it cannot run on Workers. No pricing or sizing source was collected for it here.
- STUN can be covered by free public servers, but relying on a third party's STUN is another soft dependency worth listing in the "unavoidable central parts" inventory.

### Gaps
- No capacity numbers (peers per GB of RAM, announces per second) for the reference Node tracker were found in its README; the frequently quoted figures for wt-tracker were not visible in the lines I retrieved.
- Free public TURN options and their limits in 2026 were not researched; neither was the Workers Paid base fee.
- I did not verify Durable Objects' per-object memory billing figure or WebSocket hibernation idle threshold in this session.
- Whether Cloudflare's free tier terms permit this kind of signaling use at scale was not checked.

## 8. Clear-eyed assessment: what a fork would have to change, and whether it is justified

### Takeaway
A fork is not justified by the evidence: the features this application lacks in WebTorrent (mutable state, gossip, tracker-independent discovery) are either addable through public extension points without touching the core or are architectural (one swarm and one set of connections per info hash) and would amount to a rewrite. The defensible shape is option (c) for realtime gossip and sticky state, with unmodified WebTorrent (option b) loaded lazily for large blobs, sharing one self-hosted wss tracker.

### Cited Findings
- Extension points that already exist without a fork: custom BEP 10 extensions through `wire.use()`; injection of externally signaled WebRTC peers through `torrent.addPeer(simplePeerInstance)`; custom chunk stores through `store` / `preloadedStore`; custom web-seed connections through `addWebSeed(conn)`; custom ICE servers through tracker `rtcConfig`; custom tracker lists per torrent. - [docs/api.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/api.md)
- Missing in the browser and not on any visible roadmap: DHT, PEX (issue #1191), BEP 44, BEP 46 (issue #886, open since 2016, last activity 2023-06-25), BEP 52. - [docs/bep_support.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/bep_support.md); [webtorrent issue #886](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/issues/886)
- Upstream change velocity: no human code commit among the last 25 commits on master (2026-07-23 to 2026-11-06); v3.0.0's only breaking change was the Node 22 requirement. - [commit history](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/commits/master); [v3.0.0 release](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/releases/tag/v3.0.0)
- Size comparison: WebTorrent browser bundle 55,969 bytes Brotli (measured) versus about 42.6 KB Brotli for Trystero's tracker-based strategy, which includes encrypted signaling, chunked binary transfer, media streams, identities and signed state. - [jsDelivr: webtorrent@3.0.21 dist](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/webtorrent@3.0.21/dist/webtorrent.min.js); [docs/signaling.md](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero/blob/main/docs/signaling.md)
- Precedent: bugout (full WebTorrent client plus custom wire gossip) stopped releasing in 2022; P2PT (tracker only) had one burst in 2025; Trystero (tracker as one of six interchangeable signaling strategies) shipped six releases between May and November 2026. - [npm registry: bugout](https://registry.npmjs.org/bugout); [npm registry: p2pt](https://registry.npmjs.org/p2pt); [npm registry: trystero](https://registry.npmjs.org/trystero)
- Hard platform limits that no fork can remove: 500 peer connections per page in Chromium; no UDP or TCP sockets in browsers, hence no Mainline DHT; a signaling rendezvous is always required. - [rtc_peer_connection.cc](https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/main/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/peerconnection/rtc_peer_connection.cc); [docs/bep_support.md](https://github.com/webtorrent/webtorrent/blob/master/docs/bep_support.md)

### Inferences
- What a fork would actually have to change, and whether a fork is needed for it:
  - Gossip and chat: not needed. A `wire.use()` extension on a lobby torrent, or plain data channels signaled through the tracker, both work today.
  - Sticky mutable state: not needed. Signed, sequence-numbered records (the BEP 44 data model) can ride the gossip channel; WebTorrent's core never has to know.
  - Mutable blobs: not needed. A signed pointer to the newest info hash (BEP 46's data model) carried by the same gossip, then `client.add(infoHash)`.
  - Tracker-independent discovery (browser PEX with WebRTC signaling relayed by existing peers): possible without a fork by relaying offers over a custom extension and calling `torrent.addPeer(simplePeer)`, though it would be cleaner inside the core. This is the strongest candidate for a small upstream patch rather than a fork.
  - Sharing one peer connection across many torrents, or decoupling the mesh from info hashes: this cuts against the BitTorrent wire model (one handshake per info hash per connection) and would be a rewrite of the peer and swarm layers. This is the only change that truly requires a fork, and it is also the one that makes the result no longer WebTorrent-compatible.
  - Trickle ICE or more than 5 offers per announce: changes in `bittorrent-tracker`'s client, not in WebTorrent; trivially patchable or avoidable by using one's own signaling client.
  - Bundle size reduction: achievable by not loading WebTorrent until a blob is requested (dynamic import), without a fork.
- Option (a), fork: pays the full maintenance cost of a 223 KB codebase with a one-person WebRTC dependency, to gain things that are reachable from outside. Only justified if the goal is multiplexing many torrents over a shared mesh, and then it is a new protocol.
- Option (b), unmodified WebTorrent for everything: works, and bugout is the existence proof, but every visitor pays for a torrent client and a dummy swarm just to chat, the lobby torrent's 55-connection default and the per-torrent connection model count against the 500 cap, and the approach has no maintained exemplar in 2026.
- Option (c), tracker and data channels only: matches what the maintained ecosystem converged on. Its cost is that blob distribution (piece verification, rarest-first, resumable storage, range streaming) must be either reimplemented or delegated. Delegating it to unmodified WebTorrent with web seeds, loaded on demand, gives a combined (c)+(b) design in which the two layers share the tracker and the signed-pointer records and nothing is forked.
- For small blobs the simplest correct answer may be no swarm at all: content-addressed files on the static host, with hashes announced over gossip. WebTorrent earns its place for the larger audio and weight files where peer offload matters.
- Whichever option is chosen, the same three central dependencies remain and should be owned: a wss tracker or relay (self-hosted, with public trackers as fallback), STUN/TURN, and an always-available copy of each blob (web seed). The owner's "no mandatory central server beyond what is unavoidable" is satisfiable, but "unavoidable" includes all three in practice.

### Gaps
- No public benchmark compares a `wire.use()` gossip channel with a plain data-channel mesh for latency or overhead.
- I did not prototype relayed signaling through `addPeer(simplePeer)` to confirm it works unmodified in WebTorrent 3.0.21 in the browser; it is documented API, not a tested path here.
- Whether the WebTorrent maintainers would accept a browser PEX or mutable-pointer contribution in 2026 is unknown given the absence of recent human review activity.
- The sizing claim that many concurrent torrents would hit the 500-connection cap depends on connections not being shared across torrents, which I inferred from the protocol design and did not confirm in source.
